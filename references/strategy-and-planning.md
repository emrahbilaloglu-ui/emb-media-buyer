# Strategy and planning

Build media strategy from the business constraint and customer decision, then assign channels, campaigns, creative, measurement, and budget roles. Account structure is implementation, not strategy.

## Strategy hierarchy

Define in order:

1. business outcome, acquisition unit, economics, cash/payback, capacity, and risk appetite;
2. market/customer problem, demand state, buying cycle, proof needs, and barriers;
3. growth mechanism: capture existing demand, create/shape demand, reactivate, retain/expand, launch, defend, or learn;
4. media/channel role and credible measurement method;
5. offer, destination, product/feed, sales/operations, and creative dependencies;
6. portfolio architecture, budget scenarios, tests, governance, and decision rules.

Do not start with a preferred platform or inherited campaign template. If the business constraint is stock, margin, lead handling, retention, offer, or tracking, more media may be the wrong intervention.

## Market and customer evidence

Use first-party evidence where available:

- commerce/product/search/site/app behavior;
- CRM stages, sales calls, win/loss reasons, objections, and service capacity;
- customer research, support, returns, reviews, complaints, and repeat behavior;
- category/query demand, seasonality, price/promotion, distribution, stock, and competitor propositions;
- prior creative concepts, destinations, audiences, products, markets, and actual delivered results.

Public search trends, ad libraries, competitor ads, social content, and creator commentary generate hypotheses. They do not reveal competitor objectives, profitability, budgets, or incrementality.

Separate customer, buyer, user, gatekeeper, and account when they differ. For multi-market plans, verify product availability, language, offer, regulation, cultural meaning, currency, payment, shipping/service, and measurement per market.

## Demand map

Map opportunities across:

- existing versus new customer;
- branded versus non-branded/category/problem demand;
- high-intent capture versus discovery/consideration;
- product/category/market maturity;
- prospecting, retargeting, retention, cross-sell, and reactivation;
- short versus long sales cycle;
- known versus unknown audience identity;
- supply/capacity constraints and strategic products.

Do not call branded search, retargeting, marketplace capture, or existing-customer revenue prospecting merely because the platform campaign has an acquisition label.

## Channel role selection

Evaluate each channel on:

- access to the relevant demand or exposure opportunity;
- customer/context fit and creative/format fit;
- controllability, scale, auction/supply depth, and market coverage;
- outcome signal quality, feedback latency, attribution, and experiment options;
- marginal economics, fees, production/operations cost, and capacity;
- privacy, policy, brand safety, fraud, rights, and platform dependency;
- strategic learning value and how evidence transfers—or does not transfer—to other channels.

Assign an explicit role such as demand capture, demand creation, retargeting, launch, retention, experimentation, or coverage. A channel can have several campaigns with different roles; keep economics and measurement separate when the roles differ.

## Portfolio and architecture

Architecture should make controls, economics, ownership, and learning legible while giving the platform enough eligible demand to operate. Choose consolidation or segmentation from:

- different business targets, markets, products/margins, inventories, offers, destinations, or conversion goals;
- budget and bid control needs;
- creative/customer hypotheses;
- experiment isolation and reporting grain;
- policy, language, sales/capacity, or data-governance constraints;
- platform delivery evidence and cost of fragmentation.

Do not consolidate cells that need distinct economics or governance. Do not fragment merely to preserve legacy reporting or micro-control. Platform automation is an implementation capability, not a business objective.

## Media plan contract

For each portfolio cell record:

- channel, market, campaign role, business outcome, optimization event, and source of truth;
- customer/demand hypothesis, offer, destination, product/feed, and creative concept coverage;
- budget scenario, bid/control logic, expected range, marginal assumption, and capacity;
- primary metric, guardrails, attribution, causal measurement plan, lag, and decision rule;
- owner, launch gate, dependencies, monitoring, cooldown, and rollback;
- unknowns and the evidence that would change allocation.

Budget totals must reconcile across cells, currency, period, agency/platform/production/data fees, taxes where relevant, and reserves for testing or incidents.

## Roadmap sequencing

Sequence by dependency and decision value:

1. repair material tracking, feed, destination, offer, stock/capacity, or CRM defects;
2. establish business targets and comparable baselines;
3. remove waste that is both verified and reversible;
4. restore sufficient creative/customer/demand coverage;
5. run the highest-value feasible experiments;
6. stage allocation changes and collect complete outcome windows;
7. calibrate portfolio models and institutionalize learnings.

Use 30/60/90-day phases only when they fit the business cycle. Each phase needs owner, deliverable, success/guardrail, dependency, and decision—not a list of activities.

## Forecast and downside

Every plan includes downside/base/upside scenarios, response assumption, lag, seasonality, mix, capacity, cannibalization, fees, and uncertainty. Show the switch point and the staged alternative. A channel plan is not credible if it assumes every cell can simultaneously retain its average return while scaling.
